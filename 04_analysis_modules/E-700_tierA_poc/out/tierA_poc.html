Does Tier A work on the real graph?

We cut one region out of the national graph, routed every 2017 passenger train that runs through it, and ran all six Tier A analyses for real. Five work now. One works but its detour figures depend on fixing a few known topology defects first. The tests also found those defects, which is useful in itself.

The test region

Delhi – Agra – Kanpur – Lucknow – Moradabad

Box from 77.0°E to 81.2°E and 26.3°N to 29.0°N. It holds the Delhi–Kanpur trunk, the Delhi–Agra line, the Moradabad–Bareilly–Lucknow line and the branches between them. It has dense junctions, real alternative routes and very heavy passenger traffic, so if the method breaks it should break here.

Verdicts

Six tests, one row each

1 · Routing trains on track

The base everything else needs
Works

All 1,421 trains routed. Median length error against timetable km is 3.3%; 75% of trains are within 5%. Graph routes run 2.8% long on average. Main corridors match closely: Moradabad–Lucknow 328.9 vs 326 km, Kanpur–Lucknow 74.0 vs 74 km, Ghaziabad–Moradabad 141.5 vs 141 km.

Error is lowest (2.1%) where few edge lengths came from the timetable and highest where many did. So timetable-derived lengths are not what makes the routes match.

2 · Trains per section

Where the passenger load is
Works

91% of sections carry at least one train. The busiest is Shahdara–Sahibabad–Ghaziabad at about 395 trains, then Lucknow Jn–Lucknow at 263. Our counts agree with the build's own service_load (Spearman 0.82). This is relative passenger load only. It is not utilisation, because there is no capacity or freight data.

3 · Criticality

Which section or junction failures hurt most
Works, with fixes

The removal test runs for all 690 sections that carry trains, plus 10 named junctions. It splits sections into two kinds. 36 sections strand trains outright, and all of them are single-line branches such as Barhan–Etah, Sambhal, Dhaulpur and the Gwalior narrow-gauge line. The rest force detours. The worst is the Ganga crossing at Kanpur, where 192 trains face about 560 km each.

Weighting by trains changes the answer. None of the top 20 sections by train impact are in the top 20 by plain betweenness. Two cautions: the box overstated stranding 4.6 times until each case was rechecked on the national graph, and the trunk ranking is distorted by the Aligarh defect below.

4 · Accessibility

Travel time to the nearest major hub
Works

Hubs were taken from the timetable: Kanpur, New Delhi, Lucknow, Ghaziabad, Nizamuddin, Old Delhi, Moradabad and Mathura. Median time to a hub is 74 minutes. 43% of stations are within an hour and 72% within two. By state, Delhi's median is 6 minutes, Uttar Pradesh 79 and Madhya Pradesh 154. The worst-served stations sit on the Mailani–Dudhwa–Palia branch, 4.4 to 6.3 hours out. 99 stations in the graph have no stopping train in 2017.

5 · Circuity

Network km ÷ straight-line km
Works

We checked 8,847 pairs among 142 well-served stations. Median circuity is 1.19 and 6% of pairs exceed 1.5. No pair comes out below 1.0, so no lengths are impossibly short. The outliers are all one real gap: Farrukhabad, Fatehgarh and Kannauj have no rail crossing of the Ganga toward Hardoi and Shahjahanpur, so a 46 km hop becomes 315 km. That is the kind of result this test is for.

6 · Running times from the timetable

Replacing estimated speeds
Works

69% of sections, covering 73% of route km, have an observed median running time. The median observed speed is 49 km/h, with 80% of sections between 24 and 75 km/h. Only 17 values are implausible. The current modelled times are about half the observed times (ratio 0.49), so time-based results built on modelled speeds would be badly optimistic. Use observed times.

What the tests found in the graph

Using the graph is also a way to audit it

Aligarh Jn is off the trunk

The shortest Ghaziabad–Kanpur path skips Aligarh. The trunk runs Daud Khan → Mandrak directly, and Aligarh hangs off Daud Khan as a side stub. Trains that don't stop there never pass it, so removing Aligarh affects only 99 trains, against about 200 at Hathras. This is the spine shortcutting problem in LIMITATIONS.md §1.1, showing up on the busiest line in the region. It needs an override.

27 missing-link candidates

These are consecutive stops where the graph route is more than 25% and 5 km longer than the timetable. Top cases: Dhaulpur–Morena (16 trains, 16 km vs 27 km), Tundla–Mitawal, Sarayan–Babupur, Kartauli–Ghatpuri and Achalganj–Band Hamirpur. The full list is in missing_link_candidates.csv, ready to check against audit list 07.

Delhi–Kanpur and Delhi–Agra run about 4% long

Ghaziabad–Kanpur is 421.6 km against a timetable median of 405. New Delhi–Agra Cantt is 197.6 against 190. The other main corridors are within 1%. Likely causes are the Tundla approach routed via Etmadpur, a 28.8 km lower-bound Somna–Daud Khan chord, and the Hathras Jn–Hathras Road link.

Region boxes need a national recheck

165 sections looked like they stranded trains inside the box. Only 36 do on the national graph. Any regional study has to confirm stranding on the full graph, which this run does automatically.

What this means for scope

Recommendation

  • Go national with tests 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 as they are. The method holds up on a hard region.
  • Make criticality the headline, but fix the trunk first. Correct Aligarh, Tundla and Hathras with OVERRIDES.csv, then check the 27 missing-link candidates. That is a few days of focused audit, limited to the edges trains actually use.
  • Always recheck stranding on the national graph, and report strand counts and detour costs separately.
  • Use observed running times for any time-based result. Modelled times are off by about 2×.
  • Run the national version next. The same script scales: this region took 18 seconds.
